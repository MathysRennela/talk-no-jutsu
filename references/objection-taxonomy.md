# Objection taxonomy

Severity depends on the claim's role and scope. The same issue may be minor
in an exploratory note and critical in a central theorem or safety claim.

| Level | Meaning | Typical disposition |
|---|---|---|
| Critical | Invalidates a central claim, result, or essential condition | Blocking |
| Major | Materially weakens conclusion, generality, or interpretation | Investigate promptly |
| Moderate | Worth checking, not necessarily a blocker | Defer or test |
| Minor | Limited consequence for main result | Record and defer |
| Cosmetic | Style or preference | Ignore unless relevant |

## Objection record
- Target: exact claim, step, assumption, or result.
- Argument: why it may be wrong or incomplete.
- Evidence: source, derivation, calculation, or counterexample.
- Impact: what changes if the objection is correct.
- Severity: critical, major, moderate, minor, cosmetic.
- Check: smallest useful resolution action.
- Owner and status: unresolved, resolved, accepted risk, deferred, blocking.

## Do not confuse
Novelty with importance; possibility with plausibility; missing detail with
false conclusion; style with methodological flaw; confidence with evidence;
repetition with independent corroboration.

Prioritize by impact, plausibility, cost to check, whether already addressed,
and whether resolution changes the next action. Avoid pseudo-precise scores
unless a justified scoring model exists.
